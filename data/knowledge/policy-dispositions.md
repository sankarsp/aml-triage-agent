# Internal Policy: Alert Disposition and SAR Narrative Standards

## Disposition standards
Every alert must be closed with a written rationale that references the evidence reviewed. Close an alert only when the activity is explained by information the institution holds (customer profile, documentation, prior reviewed cases). An unexplained deviation from the expected profile combined with a recognised typology must be escalated to the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) for a SAR decision. The automated agent recommends; a qualified analyst approves or overrides every disposition, and overrides require a comment.

## SAR narrative standards
Narratives must be factual, chronological and self-contained. Cover who is involved (names, identifiers, relationships), what instruments and amounts were used, when the activity occurred (date range), where (branches, jurisdictions, accounts), why the activity is suspicious (the typology and the deviation from expected behaviour), and how the activity was conducted. Do not speculate about guilt, do not include the analyst's opinions beyond the basis for suspicion, and never disclose the existence of a SAR to the customer.

## Timeliness
In the US, a SAR must generally be filed within 30 calendar days of initial detection of facts that may constitute a basis for filing. Escalated alerts must reach the FIU within 5 business days of alert creation.
