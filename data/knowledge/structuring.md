# Typology: Structuring and Smurfing

## Description
Structuring is breaking a larger sum into several smaller cash transactions so that each one stays below a reporting or record-keeping threshold (for example the USD 10,000 currency transaction report threshold in the US). Smurfing is the same technique carried out through multiple people or multiple branches. Structuring is itself an offence in many jurisdictions even when the underlying funds are legitimate.

## Red flags
- Repeated cash deposits just below the reporting threshold, often 80-99% of it, over days or weeks.
- Deposits made at different branches or ATMs on the same or consecutive days.
- Customer asks about reporting thresholds or reduces a deposit after being told a report is required.
- Cash volume inconsistent with the business type (for example a consulting firm depositing cash).
- Cash deposits followed soon after by wires or large withdrawals.

## Investigation steps
Compare cash activity to the expected cash intensity of the stated business. Review deposit locations and times. Check whether the customer has a history of currency transaction reports and whether the pattern started after one was filed. Request an explanation of the source of cash where appropriate, without tipping off the customer about a possible report.
